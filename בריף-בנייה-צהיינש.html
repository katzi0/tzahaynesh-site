02

Canonical terms

These are load-bearing. Copy that contradicts them is wrong however well it reads. The full glossary lives in CONTEXT.md at the repo root.

ענווה תרבותית / Cultural Humility

Her central term and the thread linking all four tracks. Her definition: a way of meeting any human being — curiosity toward their story, identity, values and belongings, together with a willingness to examine one's own assumptions, values and power relations in the encounter.

Do not write it as expertise about populations

This is the single most likely copy mistake, and she pre-empted it herself in the questionnaire. Cultural humility is not knowledge about "other communities", and the site must not reduce her to a specialist in any particular population. It is a stance toward difference between any two people — including the professional's own blind spots.

Also avoid "intercultural awareness" as the headline framing. That was the previous joint venture's language, not hers.

עבודה בינתרבותית / intercultural work — the applied practice. A significant and distinguishing domain, deliberately not the whole identity.

The brand question

The one live risk in this project. Recorded in full because it is likely to resurface, and because the person building the About page needs to know it exists.

Two accounts that cannot both hold

The commissioner's brief: the previous joint venture is not named anywhere. The new site is separate and replaces her presence there. Stated at the outset and reaffirmed when challenged.

Her questionnaire answers: she describes herself as its founder and manager, in the present tense and the first person singular; lists it first among her significant stations; gives its shared address as a contact email for the new site; and, asked which sites she likes, names its website as her style reference.

The existing public site credits two women as co-founders — מייסדות, plural feminine.

How it is handled

The body of work is hers and stays in the biography — roughly twenty years of development, consulting and training in intercultural work. Only the name is omitted. The affected sentence is isolated into a single content field so that restoring the name is a one-line edit rather than a rewrite.

Before launch, confirm with her directly. Publishing her as sole founder of something a live public page currently credits to two people is the kind of thing that becomes a dispute once search engines index it. This is a question for the two of them, not a copy decision.

The five tracks

Audience, format and scope are hers; do not invent offerings she did not name. The count is five rather than four for a reason recorded below and in docs/adr/0002-five-tracks-not-four.md.

She gave two different four-way splits

Her one-line summary lists therapy · consultation and parent guidance · teaching and training · intercultural lectures. Her detailed service list gives therapy · supervision and consultation for therapists · lectures and training · organizations and academia. Parent guidance appears only in the first; clinical supervision only in the second.

Both are real services, so the site carries both — five tracks. Confirm the parent-guidance track with her, since she did not include it when writing the list out in full.

Never label a nav item bare הדרכה. Hebrew uses that one word for supervising therapists and for guiding parents. Unqualified, it sends a parent to a page written for clinicians — at exactly the moment the visitor is choosing a direction.

Testimonials

She answered "כן, אצרף בהמשך" without saying from whom. This needs a boundary before the section is designed.

Institutional testimonials only

Quotes from a principal, a team leader or a training coordinator are ordinary professional marketing. Quotes from therapy clients are not: soliciting them reaches into the therapeutic relationship, and professional ethics codes treat them cautiously or bar them outright.

Design the section for named, role-attributed quotes — "מנהלת בית ספר", "מדריכה בתוכנית הכשרה" — and do not build a client-review pattern. Put this to her directly; she may never have meant clients.

Exclusions

Stated by her, and firm.

  • No children. She treats adolescents and adults only.
  • No reduction to one thing — not to a single population, not to lectures alone, not to cultural humility alone. The site must hold the full professional breadth.
  • Professional register. Personal story only where it genuinely explains the work. No private-life disclosure that does not serve professional understanding.

Design direction

She chose four atmospheres at once — warm and personal, calm and clean, professional and authoritative, artistic and creative. They are not contradictory so much as unprioritised, so the ordering below is a proposal, not her instruction.

  • Calm and clean carries the structure — generous space, restrained palette, clear type hierarchy. This is what makes a seven-audience site legible.
  • Warm and personal comes from the photography — her portrait, the workshops, the therapy room. Warmth through people rather than through decoration.
  • Authority comes from specificity — real formats, real scopes, real credentials, stated plainly. Not from visual heaviness.
  • Artistic enters once, deliberately — one considered gesture rather than creative treatment throughout. Her background is drama therapy and theatre; there is something real to draw on.

One caution on her reference. Asked which sites she likes, she named the existing joint site. Given section 04, inheriting its visual language would work against the separation this project exists to create. Worth a direct conversation about what she likes about it — warmth? clarity? the photography? — rather than reproducing the look.

09

Stack

Astro + Decap CMS, static, git-backed. Chosen against the alternatives for one reason: she said editing the site herself matters to her, and she intends to keep publishing articles. A static site she cannot touch would fail within months.

  • Astro — static output, no client JS by default, good RTL handling, content collections for articles.
  • Decap CMS — free, git-backed, browser admin. She edits through a form; the site rebuilds itself.
  • Hosting — Netlify or Vercel free tier. Build on push.

Decap's admin UI is English-only. This is the known weak point of the choice. Mitigate by writing every field label and help text in Hebrew in the config, keeping the field set small, and giving her one short walkthrough. Budget real time for this — it is the difference between a site she maintains and a site she abandons.

Accessibility

Israeli regulations require businesses serving the public to make websites accessible — IS 5568, which adopts WCAG 2.0 AA — and to publish a הצהרת נגישות. Whether a private therapy practice is in scope is a question for a qualified adviser and has not been confirmed.

Build to AA regardless, and ship the statement page. On a static Hebrew content site this is nearly free at the outset — semantic markup, sufficient contrast, visible keyboard focus, real alt text, correct heading order, no meaning carried by colour alone — and expensive to retrofit. Recorded in docs/adr/0003-accessibility-to-wcag-aa.md.
